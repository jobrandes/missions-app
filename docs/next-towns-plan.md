# Next towns plan (Oct 26, 2026)

Draft for Jo-Anne to react to. Stop ideas are starting points from general knowledge, not yet checked against Wikimedia Commons or the Library of Congress. Nothing near Sparks (no Fernley, Truckee, Minden and so on, per Jo-Anne).

## How a town earns a slot

1. **Free photos exist on both sides.** A public-domain or CC "then" photo (Library of Congress, NARA, state archives, Commons) and a free "now" photo for at least 3 of 4 stops. Photos are the number one rule.
2. **Four walkable stops** within a short drive or walk, each with a plaque, building or view you can stand in front of.
3. **Facts that can be sourced** twice (the Fact or fiction claims need 2+ https sources).
4. **A reason a stranger would care.** One famous story plus one surprise.
5. **Fits a trail** (Train Stations, Capitols and Courthouses, etc.) so it pulls players deeper.

## Tier 3: outside the US (after the iPhone launch)

The rule from the brief: nothing tied to one town or to an English idiom. These need the Spanish, French or Italian name and a note on pronunciation for any stop whose name is not obvious. Photos are the strength here, because public-domain city photography is huge.

| City | Why | Photo strength |
|---|---|---|
| Paris, France | Instantly recognizable, Atget and Marville photographs are public domain | Excellent |
| Rome, Italy | Layers of history in one walk | Excellent |
| Mexico City, Mexico | Relates to Nevada and Southwest history (Spanish and Mexican period) | Good |
| Quebec City, Canada | Walled city, English and French story, easy for US travelers | Good |
| London, UK | Many players know the stories, so Fact or fiction works well | Excellent |
| Lisbon, Portugal | 1755 earthquake before and after | Good |

For international towns the app also needs: metric distances (it already shows km and miles), a language-friendly pronunciation line, and checking that location permission wording still reads right outside the US.
